Project 02 · Process R&D · 2005 — present

Lab-to-GMP scale-up

Multi-employer expertise translating API processes from gram scale through multi-kilogram GMP manufacturing — yield improvement, robustness, and impurity control along the way.

Sai Life Sciences·Orchid Pharma·BSAZ Biotech·PharmaBlock

§ 1The scale-up problem

Reactions that work cleanly at 100 mg often fail at 100 kg. Heat removal becomes rate-limiting, mixing is no longer instantaneous, addition rates start to matter, and impurities that were invisible at lab scale become specification-defining at pilot scale. Bridging this gap reliably is the core of process chemistry — and the focus of my work across multiple employers from 2005 onwards.

Process characterization at lab scale (identify critical process parameters via DOE), risk assessment for each unit operation, demonstration runs at intermediate scale to confirm scaling rules, tech-transfer package for the manufacturing site (process description, ranges, in-process controls, analytical methods), and on-site support during first GMP campaigns.

Project 04 · Process R&D · BSAZ Biotech · 2015–2017

Dasabuvir — high-purity API

As Chief Scientist at BSAZ Biotech, established a simple, efficient final-stage purification for Dasabuvir delivering >99.8% API purity — without chromatography, through crystallization-driven rejection of impurities.

Chief Scientist·Dasabuvir > 99.8%·Chromatography-free

§ 1The purity bar

Dasabuvir is a non-nucleoside NS5B inhibitor used in HCV combination therapies (ABT-333) — and notoriously demanding to purify cleanly at scale. The difference between 99.0% and 99.85% purity often decides whether a process is commercially viable. Chromatography to clean up a final-step impurity may work at gram scale but is rarely affordable at production scale. The challenge: get the purity by chemistry and crystallization, not by column.

§ 3Workflow

Spike studies to understand which impurities rejected well from which solvent systems. Crystallization mapping — temperature, supersaturation, seeding — to land on a robust crystallization that gave both the right polymorph and the right purity. Iterated through several rounds with HPLC and ICP-MS feedback until the process reliably gave >99.8% material run after run.

Latanoprost — process & scale-up

Process research and scale-up of Latanoprost (Xalatan) — a prostaglandin F2α analog used to lower intraocular pressure in glaucoma — from lab to multi-hundred-gram scale, with full stereochemical and functional-group integrity preserved.

Research Associate·5 stereocenters·Glaucoma therapeutic

§ 1Why Latanoprost is a serious process challenge

Latanoprost is an isopropyl ester of a prostaglandin F2α analog with a saturated (15R)-hydroxyl ω-chain and a phenyl-terminus on the lipophilic tail. The molecule has five stereocenters on a cyclopentane core, a sensitive allylic alcohol, and a hydrolytically vulnerable isopropyl ester — none of which tolerate sloppy chemistry. Multi-step prostaglandin synthesis traditionally relies on protected Corey lactone intermediates with low-temperature carbonyl chemistry and chromatography-heavy purification. Project 08 · Analytical · Orchid Pharma · 2011–2015

Esomeprazole Mg — OOS investigation

Troubleshooting out-of-specification (OOS) results encountered during stability studies of Esomeprazole magnesium dihydrate and trihydrate — including hydrate-form characterization, impurity attribution, and root-cause analysis under ICH Q1A conditions.

Sr. Research Associate·Stability ICH Q1A·Proton pump inhibitor

§ 1The problem

Esomeprazole is the (S)-enantiomer of omeprazole — a proton pump inhibitor (Nexium) marketed as the magnesium salt, with both dihydrate and trihydrate forms in commercial use. The molecule's sulfoxide is acid- and base-labile, prone to disproportionation, racemization, and oxidation. During ICH stability studies, the API showed OOS behavior — degradation impurities exceeding specification at intermediate or accelerated conditions. The job: identify what was failing, why, and what to change.

§ 2Investigation toolkit

§ 3Workflow

Began with a systematic review of the failing batches — analytical method performance, container-closure integrity, environmental exposure. Ran forced-degradation studies to identify which stress conditions reproduced the observed impurities, confirming the route of degradation. Characterized the dihydrate vs. trihydrate samples by PXRD, TGA, and DSC to verify that the correct hydrate had been packed — and that hydrate interconversion under elevated humidity was not driving the OOS. Proposed corrective actions on the analytical method and container-closure specification.

Project 09 · Inorganic · Materials Technology · 2017

Ag-doped CaSiO₃ bioceramic

Sol-gel combustion synthesis of silver-doped wollastonite for biomedical applications — apatite formation, antibacterial action, and cytocompatibility with osteoblast-like cells.

Materials Technology (2017)·DOI: 10.1080/10667857.2017.1389050

§ 1Motivation

Wollastonite (β-CaSiO₃) is one of the most promising bioactive calcium silicates for bone regeneration — it forms a chemical bond with natural bone through rapid apatite layer growth in body fluid. However, after implantation pure wollastonite offers little resistance to bacterial colonization, which can cause infection and force a second surgery. This study introduced silver as a dopant to give the ceramic intrinsic antibacterial activity without losing its bioactivity, prepared by a novel sol-gel combustion route using glycine as a fuel.

§ 2Synthesis & characterization stack

Synthesis
Sol-gel combustion, glycine fuel
Compositions
Ca₁₋ₓAgₓSiO₃ (x = 0, 1, 3, 5 wt%)
Structural
ATR-FTIR (Shimadzu), powder-XRD (Bruker D8)
Morphology
SEM-EDS (Hitachi-S4800), TEM-SAED (Tecnai 20G²)
Biology
SBF immersion, disc diffusion, MTT assay
Statistics
GraphPad Prism 6, two-way ANOVA

§ 3Workflow

Calcium nitrate, TEOS, and glycine were dissolved in distilled water with concentrated HNO₃ (pH ~1), stirred at 85 °C for 5 h to form a transparent gel, aged, then combusted at 400 °C and sintered at 800 °C/3 h to give phase-pure powders. In vitro bioactivity was assessed by immersion in simulated body fluid (SBF, Kokubo recipe) for 7 and 14 days at 37 °C with daily SBF replacement. Antibacterial activity was measured by zone-of-inhibition disc diffusion against E. coli and S. aureus. Cytocompatibility was evaluated by MTT assay on MG-63 osteoblast-like cells at 50–250 µg/mL for 24 and 48 h.

§ 4Antibacterial activity — zone of inhibition

§ 5Key findings

17 mm
ZoI vs E. coli (5 wt%)
114-119%

Powder-XRD confirmed pure single-phase formation matching JCPDS 43-1460 across all compositions. Crystallite size dropped from 75 nm (pure) to 38 nm at 5 wt% Ag, indicating that silver disrupts long-range ordering. SBF immersion produced ball-like apatite grains within 7 days that progressed to needle-like crystalline HAP by day 14, with Ca/P ratio ≈ 1.67 — close to natural bone. Antibacterial zones grew with Ag content (10 → 17 mm against E. coli, 9 → 15 mm against S. aureus), and E. coli was the more susceptible strain due to its thinner cell wall. Critically, the 5 wt% Ag composition was non-cytotoxic to MG-63 cells across all concentrations and timepoints — viability actually increased to 114% (24 h) and 119% (48 h) at 250 µg/mL.

§ 6Significance

First report of Ag-doped CaSiO₃ via sol-gel combustion using glycine as fuel. The combination of bioactivity (apatite formation), antibacterial action (driven by Ag⁺ release and interaction with microbial DNA), and non-toxicity to bone-forming cells positions the material as a viable candidate for infection-resistant orthopedic and dental implant coatings.

Project 10 · Inorganic · J. Inorg. Organomet. Polym. · 2018

Zn-doped CaSiO₃ bioceramic

Zinc-substituted wollastonite for bone tissue engineering — porous nanostructure, antibacterial action against gram-positive and gram-negative pathogens, and good MG-63 osteoblast compatibility.

J. Inorg. Organomet. Polym. Mater. (2018)·DOI: 10.1007/s10904-018-0922-8

§ 1Motivation

Zinc is naturally present in human bone as a trace element and plays a direct role in bone metabolism and tissue regeneration. Substituting Zn²⁺ into CaSiO₃ couples bioactivity (apatite formation) with antibacterial behavior and biologically meaningful ion release. This work extended the sol-gel combustion approach to zinc dopants, examining how Zn content modulates structure, porosity, biology, and ion release.

§ 2Synthesis & characterization stack

Synthesis
Sol-gel combustion, glycine fuel
Compositions
Ca₁₋ₓZnₓSiO₃ (x = 0, 1, 3, 5 wt%)
Structural
ATR-FTIR, powder-XRD (Bruker D8)
Thermal & size
TGA (EXSTAR TG/DTA), DLS (Horiba SZ-100)
Morphology
SEM-EDS (Hitachi-S4800), HR-TEM (Tecnai 20G²)
Biology
SBF immersion, CFU antibacterial, MTT on MG-63

§ 3Workflow

Ca(NO₃)₂·4H₂O, Zn(NO₃)₂, and TEOS were dissolved in distilled water with glycine as fuel; HNO₃ was added (pH ~1) to drive silanol formation and reactivity. The mixture was stirred at 85 °C for 5 h, dried, combusted at 400 °C for 2 h, and sintered at 800 °C for 3 h. In vitro bioactivity was tested by 14-day SBF immersion with daily medium replacement. Antibacterial activity was quantified by colony forming unit (CFU) counting against E. coli and S. aureus, while cytocompatibility was probed by MTT assay on MG-63 cells at 200–1000 µg/mL for 24 h.

§ 4Antibacterial activity — CFU reduction

§ 5Key findings

~90%

118%

XRD confirmed phase-pure formation matching JCPDS 43-1460, with crystallite size decreasing from 128 nm (pure) to 42 nm at 5 wt% Zn — a clear sign of Zn-induced lattice distortion. TGA showed three distinct weight-loss steps (water, nitrates, organics) with phase formation initiating at ~760 °C. SEM and TEM revealed that Zn substitution produced increasingly porous, aggregated morphology, while DLS gave hydrodynamic sizes of 108–166 nm. After 14-day SBF immersion, 5 wt% Zn-CaSiO₃ developed dense ball-like apatite crystals on the surface. CFU counting showed dramatic microbial reduction at 5 wt% Zn (E. coli: 7.3 × 10⁶ → 5.8 × 10⁵; S. aureus: 9.8 × 10⁶ → 9.8 × 10⁵). MG-63 cells were non-toxic at 200–600 µg/mL and showed only minor reduction at 800–1000 µg/mL.

§ 6Significance

First report of Zn-doped CaSiO₃ synthesized by sol-gel combustion. The work confirmed that biologically relevant Zn²⁺ — abundant in natural bone — can be cleanly integrated into the wollastonite lattice and tuned to deliver antibacterial activity without compromising osteoblast viability. The porous architecture is a useful platform for bone tissue engineering.
